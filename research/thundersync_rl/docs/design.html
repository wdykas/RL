The problem

Synchronous on-policy GRPO alternates two phases. The engine samples a batch at weights θk; the learner computes the update; the engine gets θk+1 and starts again. Each phase leaves the other's GPUs idle, and the rollout phase ends with a long tail where a few long trajectories decode alone on an almost empty engine.

Asynchronous RL removes the idle time by letting the engine run ahead on older weights. The price is staleness: the learner trains on trajectories sampled up to L versions ago and needs importance corrections or clipping to stay stable.

This work removes both kinds of idle time without giving up exactness, in two layers:

  • Gradient streaming (the ThunderSyncRL paper) moves the learner's work into the rollout tail: each trajectory is backpropagated as soon as it finishes, at θk.
  • Cross-iteration speculation (new here) moves the engine's work for iteration k+1 into iteration k: it drafts the next batch under the current weights and verifies those drafts exactly once θk+1 exists.

Layer 1: Gradient streaming

A GRPO advantage depends on the whole group, so a trajectory's gradient seemingly cannot be used before its group finishes. But the update is linear in the per-trajectory score gradients Hi = ∇ log πθk(yi) (masked, summed over tokens):

Δ = Σ_i a_i · H_i B[g, r] = Σ_{i ∈ group g, r_i = r} H_i accumulated while group g is open Δ = Σ_g Σ_r a_g(r) · B[g, r] folded in when g closes

Every group-wise estimator gives trajectories with equal rewards in the same group the same advantage, so per-(group, reward) buckets are exact for any estimator. That includes NeMo-RL's default leave-one-out baseline with leave-one-out std, which is not affine in the reward. With binary rewards this is two buffers per open group. If a group exceeds max_buckets_per_group distinct rewards, it collapses to the paper's affine (Σ riHi, Σ Hi) form, and the accumulator raises an error if the estimator is not affine.

Normalization, the data-parallel reduction, clipping, the single optimizer step and the refit happen once per batch. Measured on Qwen2.5-0.5B in fp32: the streamed gradient differs from Policy.train by a relative 4.6–7.4 × 10−6, the same order as re-batching the synchronous update.

Scheduling

  • Final-first. Rows of closed groups (final advantages known) always go first. Idle learner time streams one open group's rows into buckets.
  • Length-sorted chunks. Final rows from different groups share micro-batches, sorted by length to limit padding, and are balanced over data-parallel ranks by tokens.
  • Zero-advantage chunks. With loss_fn.skip_zero_advantage_rows, zero-advantage rows go into chunks of their own. The worker counts their tokens for normalization and drops the whole chunk without a forward pass. Learner time: 2.04 → 1.55 s per step.
  • Expert parallelism. With expert_model_parallel_size > 1 every forward/backward runs an all-to-all over the EP group, which spans data-parallel ranks, so all ranks must run the same number of micro-batches per dispatch. The planner then pads short ranks with one-row padding chunks (fully masked: no gradient, no normalization count), and the worker ignores skip_zero_advantage_rows (zero-advantage rows run a forward). Open-group buckets are not exact under EP: a bucket is scaled by its group's advantage afterwards, assuming the rank's gradient came only from its own rows, but expert weights also get gradient from tokens other EP ranks route to them. validate_config therefore requires granularity=group (or max_open_groups=0) with EP; final chunks carry their advantages in the loss and are exact. GPU check (random 4-layer, 8-expert Qwen3-MoE, EP=2 × DP=2 per policy, fp32, uneven per-rank chunk counts): streamed and synchronous updates match (Δk ≤ 6.6 × 10−5 over 3 steps, same as a row-permuted synchronous control); with open-group buckets the expert weights diverged (Δk 0.24). Verification under EP: every scorer forward is an all-to-all over the EP group and each rank holds only its own experts, so the learner's verifiers run in lockstep (_expert_parallel_lockstep): the ranks exchange their per-batch stash versions once, each batch slot scores the union of versions in the same order, and a rank without rows for a slot or version runs a one-row dummy forward; keyed verification pads its batch count the same way. Inference-side verification is rejected under inference EP: the engine steps its EP ranks together, so a worker blocked in a scorer forward deadlocks against its peers' engine all-to-all (observed). End to end on the tiny MoE (EP=2 learner and inference, verify_on=learner): block and keyed speculation run without hangs.

Layer 2: Cross-iteration speculation

Zero staleness forces a drain at every update: iteration k+1 cannot sample until θk+1 exists. Speculation breaks that dependency without breaking exactness. While iteration k runs, the engine drafts the rollouts of the next iterations under the weights it has. After the step, each draft is verified against θk+1 with a rule whose output is distributed exactly as πθk+1. Only the rejected or unfinished remainders are decoded fresh.

Why it can work

One optimizer step moves the policy very little. The per-token rejection rate between πk and πk+1 measured in bf16 is about 1.7 × 10−3, and it is the same for drafts made one to four versions earlier. That flatness shows bf16 activation rounding dominates it, not policy change. In fp32 the true one-step drift is about 3 × 10−4 per token. Most drafted tokens are therefore acceptable, provided verification is precise enough to see the real drift through the numerical noise.

Lifecycle of one iteration

  1. Draft. From the start of iteration k, the Megatron inference engine streams drafts for the prompts of iterations k+1 … k+draft_lookahead (start_drafts). Draft requests share the engine with iteration k's own rollouts and fill the capacity its tail leaves idle. New cohorts ramp in at most two per step (new_cohort_targets).
  2. Cut at the deadline. Just before the optimizer step, unfinished drafts are aborted and snapshotted (collect_drafts). Any prefix of a draft is a valid draft. A cut draft resumes from its prefix in the next iteration, under the newer weights.
  3. Record provenance. Each draft carries segments (version, start, end) saying which weights drafted which positions, because verification needs each token's true proposal distribution.
  4. Stash weights. Still at θk, every verifier keeps a bf16 copy of the weights (stash_weights), one per version that drafted pending tokens.
  5. Verify. At iteration k+1, verifiers recompute q from the stashes and p from θk+1, block-verify each draft, and publish a plan per trajectory: the kept prefix plus one bonus or residual token.
  6. Continue. The rollout code is unchanged. SpeculativeGeneration wraps the generation interface: a fully accepted, finished draft returns immediately, and anything else is sent to the engine to continue from its verified prefix.

Block verification

Token-by-token speculative sampling rejects at the first position where the noise in p/q happens to fall low. Block verification (Sun et al. 2024) looks at the whole draft at once, so positive and negative noise in the ratio partly cancel along the block. For a draft x1..g from q, target p, and per-position distributions pi, qi:

b_0 = 1, b_i = min(1, b_{i-1} · p(x_i)/q(x_i)) → log b_i = S_i − max_{j≤i} S_j, S_i = Σ_{j≤i} log(p/q)(x_j) r_i = Σ_v max(b_i · p_i(v) − q_i(v), 0) (i < g) h_i = r_i / (r_i + 1 − b_i) (i < g), h_g = b_g τ = max{ i : η_i ≤ h_i }, η_i ~ U(0,1) independent keep x_1..x_τ, then emit y ~ p_g if τ = g (bonus token) y ~ norm(max(b_τ p_τ − q_τ, 0)) otherwise (residual)

The output (x1..τ, y) is distributed exactly as an autoregressive sample from p. tests/unit/test_block_verification.py checks this by exhaustive enumeration to a total-variation distance under 10−10.

Fused implementation

block_verify_logits works from raw logits: one logsumexp per row of p and q, the draft-token ratios and b on length-g vectors, and one Triton pass per position for the residual masses ri. Only the residual row actually sampled from is materialized. It shares acceptance_weights, stop_probabilities and emit_distribution with the reference and rounds the same way (fp32 log-probs, exponentiated in fp64); it is draw-for-draw identical to the reference in tests.

Keyed sampling (alternative mode)

verify_mode=keyed makes every token a Gumbel-max sample whose noise is a hash of (seed, absolute position, token id), computed in a Triton kernel inside the engine's sampler. Two policies then emit the same token wherever their Gumbel races agree, so a draft can be checked position by position with no q at all. It is exactly on-policy but only keeps 51–60% of tokens, so block verification is the default.

The verification scorer

Acceptance depends on log p − log q for weights one to three versions apart, and that difference is smaller than bf16 rounding noise. The verifier therefore scores with a second Megatron GPTModel built from a shallow copy of the training provider, with fp32 parameters and activations. Its parameters are refreshed by copying the training weights (about 7 ms at 1.5B). With NVIDIA_TF32_OVERRIDE=1, its GEMMs run on TF32 tensor cores. No Hugging Face model is used anywhere.

Making the fp32 forward fast

About 85% of verification time is scorer forward passes. Megatron and Transformer Engine are tuned for bf16 and fall back to slow paths in fp32. Three changes in scorer_kernels.py and worker.py:

  • Attention through torch SDPA. Replaces plain causal softmax attention only (no sliding window, no attention sinks), using each module's own softmax scale. About 2× faster per layer than the unfused fp32 path and about 1000× closer to exact (max error 1.7 × 10−6 vs 2 × 10−3 with TF32 score GEMMs).
  • Triton SwiGLU for dense MLP modules only (MoE experts keep their own forward). One pass over the fc1 output replaces strided silu, + offset and mul: 3.8× faster, relative error 4 × 10−7.
  • Selective scoring (select_positions). Each version's q forward stops at the last position that version drafted (causal attention makes the truncation exact), and the LM head runs only on positions whose logits verification needs. The p forward skips prompt positions. The logits are bitwise identical to the full forward (THUNDERSYNC_SELECT_CHECK=1).
Attention needs fp32-level accuracy. Running only attention in bf16 inside the fp32 scorer drops tokens kept from 99.2% to 96.4% and fully accepted drafts from 98.5% to 94.7%, which slows iterations from 3.31 to 4.12 s. A Triton flash-attention kernel with tf32x3 dots matched fp32 accuracy but was 2× slower than SDPA.

q storage scales with parameters, not tokens

Storing each draft's full-vocabulary q costs O(draft tokens × vocabulary), about 150k floats per token. Instead the verifiers keep one bf16 weight copy per pending version (2 bytes per parameter per model-parallel shard) and recompute q per batch. Memory is O(parameters + one batch × vocabulary).

Overlapped verification pipeline

At the start of iteration k+1 every GPU is briefly free. Verification uses all of them and overlaps with the rollouts:

  • Both pools verify (verify_on=both). Rows are split into verify_chunks chunks, assigned round-robin to the learner and inference worker groups. Rows are owned by data-parallel rank through a stable hash of the draft key. Sampling is seeded per data-parallel rank, so tensor-parallel partners make identical decisions.
  • Plans publish per chunk (verify_overlap). Rollouts wait on a condition variable for their own plan, not for the whole batch. Verification errors wake every waiting rollout and surface instead of hanging it.
  • Longest drafts first (verify_longest_first), so the continuations most likely to become stragglers start earliest.
  • Training starts as soon as groups complete. The learner trains the first verified groups while later chunks are still verifying.

Exactness

What is exact. Gradient streaming produces the synchronous update up to fp32 summation order. Block verification returns exact samples of p given each draft's true proposal distribution q. Every gradient is taken at the weights that define the sampling target, with no staleness and no importance weights.

Where an approximation enters. Drafts are sampled by the bf16 inference engine, but q is recomputed by the fp32 scorer. The two differ by numerical noise at equal weights. This is the same kind of train/inference mismatch that every RL pipeline already has (measured per-token probability ratio about 1.0035 at equal weights). Batch-invariant kernels (NeMo-RL PR 3208) would make q bitwise the sampling distribution. Keyed mode avoids the issue entirely at lower acceptance.

Reward check over 100 steps. Speculation vs a second no-speculation baseline: reward difference −0.0016 ± 0.0037, paired by step. For scale, the two baselines differ from each other by −0.0080 ± 0.0036, so run-to-run noise is larger than any effect of speculation.

Where the remaining time goes

  • Steps where every draft is accepted take about 2.0–2.3 s. Those steps are learner-bound: about 0.6 s until the first verified groups, training, and a 0.2 s step and refit.
  • About one draft per step (of 128) is rejected. Rejections occur at about 1.5 × 10−5 per token, the same for tokens drafted one or three versions earlier, so lookahead costs no acceptance. The remaining rejections reflect the true one-step policy change.
  • A rejection costs its remainder. The median remainder is about 450 tokens, decoded serially at about 3 ms per token. That straggler tail is most of the gap between the 2.3 s floor and the 3.0 s mean.
  • Generation is now near saturation. The inference GPUs are about 83% busy keeping up with drafting (about 115k drafted tokens per step); the learner GPUs about 30%.
  • The learner is CPU-bound. A training micro-batch takes about 130 ms of CPU for 70 ms of GPU work. Rounding padded lengths to multiples of 128 (policy.make_sequence_length_divisible_by=128) stopped cuDNN fused attention from building a new plan for every micro-batch: 270 → 130 ms CPU per micro-batch, learner 1.48 → 1.19 s per step. Iteration time did not move, because generation is the bottleneck.

When speculation pays

Speculation converts idle generation capacity into next-iteration rollouts. It cannot create capacity. Its gain is bounded by the gap between the zero-staleness baseline and an asynchronous pipeline.

Batch invariance (NeMo-RL PR 3208)

Branch thundersync-rl-bi applies PR 3208 (policy.megatron_cfg.zero_train_gen_mismatch): Megatron training and Megatron inference run the same batch-invariant kernel stack, so generation log-probs and training log-probs of the same tokens are bitwise equal.

How the kernels get batch invariance (te_native backend): native cuBLASLt GEMMs with a starved workspace (no split-K, so a fixed K-reduction order at every row count), batch-invariant aten::_log_softmax / mean, TE RMSNorm kept native under 64-multiple token alignment, FlashAttention 4 with num_splits=1. Requirements enforced by the PR's validator: BF16, TP=1, CP=1, no packing, generation transformer_impl=inference_optimized (so no QKV-bias models such as Qwen2.5), make_sequence_length_divisible_by a multiple of 64.

Environment. TE 2.18 (built from source against torch 2.11), flash-attn-4 4.0.0b19, CuTe DSL 4.6.2 (including nvidia-cutlass-dsl-libs-core, the Python frontend), quack-kernels 0.6.4 and apache-tvm-ffi 0.1.11, as a PYTHONPATH overlay (thundersync_work/build_bi_overlay.sh).

Speculation under BI

  • Scorer precision. BI kernels are BF16-only (TransformerConfig asserts it), so an fp32 scorer is built with batch_invariant_mode=False on its own copy, keeping the process-wide TE attention settings (its attention is replaced by SDPA anyway). A bf16 scorer would match the policy bitwise once it scores like training (training op sequence, rows padded to the training multiple, full-row LM head, training's fp32 log-softmax: 92–97% of tokens bitwise equal so far), but its rounding jitter between consecutive weight versions is part of the policy, so exact verification against it rejects long drafts: 33–44% of draft tokens kept on Qwen3-1.7B's ~3k-token outputs versus 94–98% with the fp32 scorer. The fp32 scorer is the default; rollouts then follow the fp32 approximation of the policy, as without BI.
  • Where the scorer runs. On the learner it is stable but does not scale under BI: BI requires TP=1, so each learner GPU holds the full fp32 copy (4 B/param) plus a bf16 stash per pending version on top of training state (Qwen3-8B ran out of memory on 2 and 3 learner GPUs). On the inference GPUs (verify_on=inference, the recommended BI setting) it first broke the engine: acceptance decayed step by step (97% → ~40%) and the engine eventually sampled from NaN logits (token id 155647 outside the 151936 vocab).
  • Root cause and fix. Under BI, NeMo-RL turns flashinfer RoPE off, so the engine reads its RoPE table from RotaryEmbedding.forward, an lru_cache(maxsize=32) shared by every instance in the process, and the engine's inference CUDA graphs capture that table's address. The scorer's own RoPE calls evicted the engine's entry, and the generation worker's cache_clear() (prepare/finish generation) freed it; the scorer's activations and weight stashes then reused the memory the graphs kept reading. Fixes: megatron_worker._pin_rotary_tables memoizes the engine's RoPE tables per module with no eviction (BI path; unit-tested in tests/unit/models/generation/test_megatron_rope_pin.py), and the scorer's RoPE forward bypasses the shared cache. The scorer copy also builds standard Transformer Engine layers without CUDA graphs, so both verifier pools compute identical p and the scorer never joins the engine's graph state. Result (Qwen3-1.7B, BI, verification on inference GPUs, 10 steps): 92–99% of draft tokens kept with no decay; the train/generation log-prob gap stays at the fp32-scorer level (mean 0.011) on accepted tokens and is zero on engine-decoded ones. Qwen3-8B, BI, 1 node (2 inference + 2 training GPUs, verification on inference, 4 steps): 93–99% kept, 91–95% of drafts fully accepted, about 50 s per step at ~3.5k mean generated tokens.
  • The same eviction hazard applies to any process that captures inference CUDA graphs with the non-flashinfer RoPE path and creates other RoPE models or calls cache_clear() (upstream: MCore could keep the dynamic-inference table in rotary_pos_emb_cache, as its flashinfer path does).

BI-enabled optimizations (opt-in)

Bitwise train/generation parity removes work that exists only to bridge numerics. All of these are off by default.

Fused verification + training (thundersync.fused_verify_train, fused_train.py). Experimental, opt-in; exact on GPU, not faster yet (A/B below). With BI, the learner's bf16 training forward is the engine's numerics, so p for verification can come from the training model itself at θk+1, and that p forward is exactly the forward that training of iteration k+1 needs for the same trajectory. Fused mode keeps it:

  1. q is recomputed from the weight stashes as before (bf16 scorer, no grad).
  2. p is a forward of the training model, inside the already open train step, shaped like a training micro-batch (one row, padded to make_sequence_length_divisible_by, same model_forward call). Rows whose full acceptance would make a finished rollout (EOS or length cap) run with autograd; the others without.
  3. A draft that is fully accepted and finished keeps its graph on the verifying data-parallel rank, up to fused_max_retained_tokens per rank (FusedGraphStore); every other graph is freed at once. Its plan is complete, so the rollout returns immediately and the environment rewards it through the normal rollout path; no extra reward hook is needed.
  4. The driver matches the finished trajectory to its retained forward (claim_fused), and the planner sends it as a one-row chunk pinned to that rank, into its (group, reward) bucket while the group is open or as a final row once it closed, exactly as any other trajectory. The worker runs the regular train_microbatch, with only model_forward replaced by the retained logits: same loss, token and sequence counts, bucket capture and finalization. The training row is the verified row plus the environment's reply; causal attention makes the verified logits its prefix, and the reply positions, masked out of the loss, are filled with constants that carry no gradient. Any mismatch (other tokens, an unmasked token after the verified row, a missing graph) falls back to the real forward.
  5. Retained graphs never outlive the step: the barrier drops unclaimed ones before the optimizer step.

The gradient equals the unfused path's up to fp32 summation order (per-row losses are additive, and BI makes a row's forward independent of its micro-batch). Each fused trajectory saves one full training forward of its row (fused/trajectories, fused/forward_tokens_saved; fused/retained, fused/claimed, fused/fallbacks, fused/unclaimed track the rest). It pays where bf16 verification keeps most drafts whole: short outputs, where block verification against the training-dtype policy keeps about 87–91% of tokens. On long outputs bf16 acceptance is low (33–44% of draft tokens on Qwen3-1.7B's ~3k-token outputs), few drafts finish whole, and the fp32 scorer (not fusable: its p is not the training forward) is the better choice. Requirements, enforced by validate_fused_verify_train and on the worker: speculate, verify_mode=block, verify_on=learner, verify_precision=model, batch_invariant_mode, PP=1 (and BI's TP=1, CP=1), generation temperature 1, no fused linear log-probs, no MTP or draft-model training. THUNDERSYNC_FUSED_CHECK=1 also runs the real forward for each fused row and prints the largest logit difference over the verified positions (expected 0).

GPU A/B. Qwen3-1.7B, BI, 2 + 2 GPUs, 2k-token cap, learner bf16 block verification, same seed, 6 steps (thundersync_work/fused_ab.sh): every fused forward matched a fresh forward bitwise (THUNDERSYNC_FUSED_CHECK: max |logit diff| 0), and the train/generation mismatch check read 0 on every token in every step, whereas unfused bf16 verification left 1–4% of accepted tokens inexact (its scorer copy is not the training forward). 5–13 trajectories per step were fused (10–27k forward tokens saved, no fallbacks), but steady-state steps took 13.4–14.4 s against 10.5–11.0 s unfused: rollouts wait about 3 s longer for verification forwards that run with autograd, which costs more than the training forwards they save at this size. Acceptance was the same either way (41–52% of draft tokens) and well below the fp32 scorer's 92–99%, so fused mode is a net loss here. It is worth retesting where training forwards are expensive relative to verification (large models, learner-bound runs). On Qwen3-8B (1 node, 2 + 2 GPUs) the default settings run out of memory on the learner (weight stashes, retained graphs and training state on TP=1 GPUs); it fits with draft_lookahead=1 verify_batch_tokens=4096 fused_max_retained_tokens=8192, keeping 66–68% of draft tokens, but only about one trajectory per step finishes whole and gets fused.

Keyed verification under BI (verify_mode=keyed). With bitwise parity the learner's logits for a draft are the ones the engine sampled from, so keyed verification needs no scorer copy and no weight stashes: p from the current training weights is all it reads, and the check is token-level (keep the agreeing prefix, then the learner's own token). It stays exactly on-policy, at the lower token-level acceptance of block verification. GPU check (Qwen3-1.7B, BI, 2 + 2 GPUs, thundersync_work/keyed_bi.sh): the self-test re-drafts with the same seeds and keeps 100% of every draft on unchanged weights, and the train/generation mismatch check reads 0 on every token of every step once the plan reports full-vocabulary log-probs (sampling keeps the vocab_limit slice the engine's keyed sampler uses). Acceptance across a weight update is 10–13% of draft tokens, against 92–99% for block verification with the fp32 scorer, so keyed mode trades nearly all of the speculation gain for removing the scorer and the stashes; it is the memory-light option, not the fast one. On Qwen3-8B (1 node, 2 + 2 GPUs) it fits on the learner, keeps 7–10% of draft tokens and takes 66–100 s per step against about 50 s for block verification on the inference GPUs.

No importance-sampling correction. Generation and training log-probs are bitwise equal under BI, so the importance weights are exactly 1 and loss_fn.use_importance_sampling_correction can be disabled. In fused mode the plan's log-probs are computed with training's own log-softmax from the training forward, so this holds for fully accepted speculative tokens too.

Open work

  • Stragglers. About one rejected draft per step decodes its remainder at about 3 ms per token. Only faster single-sequence decoding helps, such as engine-level speculative decoding with an MTP or EAGLE head.
  • Generation capacity. At 1.5B generation is near saturation on 2 + 2 GPUs, and a 3 + 1 split makes the learner the bottleneck; faster engine decoding is the lever.
  • Training CPU overhead. About 130 ms CPU vs 70 ms GPU per micro-batch; training CUDA graphs would need static or bucketed shapes.
  • Batch invariance at scale. Run the BI speculation presets (inference-side verification) on 2-node 8B/14B and Nemotron-Nano on the cluster (1-node 8B passes); validate the opt-in BI optimizations on GPU.
  • MoE. A real-model run; inference-side verification under EP (needs the engine paused or stepped in lockstep with the scorer); open-group buckets under EP.
  • Scaling gaps. Context parallelism in the scorer, zero-advantage skip with sequence packing.
  • Upstream. Default make_sequence_length_divisible_by to 64–128 for Megatron training.
  • Fused verification + training on GPU. Validate fused_verify_train end to end (THUNDERSYNC_FUSED_CHECK=1, gradient against the unfused path) and measure the saved learner time on short-output tasks (see Batch invariance).
